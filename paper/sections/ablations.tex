\subsection{Content versus format}
The decomposition channels (Table~\ref{tab:paired}, Fig.~\ref{fig:paired}) separate the two differences between our sentences and the basic schema.

\emph{Content.} Adding coordinates to the sentence gives a channel that is faster than the basic schema by \rhval{paired/nl+coords-minus-symbolic/all/d_steps/mean:2} [\rhval{paired/nl+coords-minus-symbolic/all/d_steps_lo/mean:2}, \rhval{paired/nl+coords-minus-symbolic/all/d_steps_hi/mean:2}] steps pooled, with the gain concentrated on relational (\rhval{paired/nl+coords-minus-symbolic/relational/d_steps/mean:2} [\rhval{paired/nl+coords-minus-symbolic/relational/d_steps_lo/mean:2}, \rhval{paired/nl+coords-minus-symbolic/relational/d_steps_hi/mean:2}]) and attribute (\rhval{paired/nl+coords-minus-symbolic/attribute/d_steps/mean:2} [\rhval{paired/nl+coords-minus-symbolic/attribute/d_steps_lo/mean:2}, \rhval{paired/nl+coords-minus-symbolic/attribute/d_steps_hi/mean:2}]) queries and exactly zero on exact and synonym queries, where there is no colour or relation to exploit. Adding colour and landmark fields to the schema gives \emph{the same numbers in every cell}: with a lossless template and parser the two channels deliver identical records, so the receiver behaves identically. Sending only the sender's confidence as one extra field recovers a similar pooled gain (\rhval{paired/symbolic+conf-minus-symbolic/all/d_steps/mean:2} [\rhval{paired/symbolic+conf-minus-symbolic/all/d_steps_lo/mean:2}, \rhval{paired/symbolic+conf-minus-symbolic/all/d_steps_hi/mean:2}]). The benefit therefore belongs to the information, not to the language format.

\emph{Location reference.} Replacing coordinates by a room name in an otherwise identical sentence costs \rhval{paired/nl-minus-nl+coords/all/d_steps/mean:2} [\rhval{paired/nl-minus-nl+coords/all/d_steps_lo/mean:2}, \rhval{paired/nl-minus-nl+coords/all/d_steps_hi/mean:2}] steps pooled, and the cost is present in every tier. The ground robot has to search the named room instead of walking to a block of cells.

Together these account for the main result: our NL channel adds content that helps on open queries and pays a room-level localisation cost on all queries.

\subsection{Team composition and a coordination failure}
Table~\ref{tab:team} varies the team. With the mutual room-avoidance rule of the main configuration, two ground robots or two aerial robots that communicate do far \emph{worse} than the same teams without a channel. Tracing episodes showed a symmetric livelock: two identical robots that start on the same cell announce the same room, both yield, announce the next room, and keep moving in lockstep, which is visible as an overlap of one. The ground-aerial team does not suffer from this because its robots differ in speed and sensing. We added, after seeing this, a priority rule in which only the second robot yields. It removes the livelock: two ground robots exchanging only intents then reach \rhval{abl_team/nl-two-ground-robots-priority-rule/mixed/steps/mean:2} steps and \rhval{abl_team/nl-two-ground-robots-priority-rule/mixed/success/mean:pct1}\% success, close to the ground-aerial NL team (\rhval{abl_team/nl-messaging/mixed/steps/mean:2} steps, \rhval{abl_team/nl-messaging/mixed/success/mean:pct1}\%). For the ground-aerial team the priority rule is slightly worse than the mutual rule for both channels and leaves their order unchanged (Table~\ref{tab:team}). Two aerial robots gain nothing from intents, since each must sweep the whole open area anyway and verification is their bottleneck.

Two honest readings follow. The heterogeneous team is not much better than a well-coordinated pair of ground robots in this world, so the value of heterogeneity here is modest. And the coordination content of a message (who goes where) mattered at least as much as its format.
