\subsection{Sanity ordering}
Table~\ref{tab:main} and Fig.~\ref{fig:tiers} show the expected ordering of the reference systems in every tier: the random-walk team almost never finds the target, the single ground robot succeeds in about half of the episodes, the two-robot team without communication is clearly better (the aerial robot helps only through its slow fallback verification), and the communicating teams are better again. Against the team without a channel, symbolic messaging changes the steps to find by \rhval{paired/symbolic-minus-no-comm/all/d_steps/mean:2} [\rhval{paired/symbolic-minus-no-comm/all/d_steps_lo/mean:2}, \rhval{paired/symbolic-minus-no-comm/all/d_steps_hi/mean:2}] and NL messaging by \rhval{paired/nl-minus-no-comm/all/d_steps/mean:2} [\rhval{paired/nl-minus-no-comm/all/d_steps_lo/mean:2}, \rhval{paired/nl-minus-no-comm/all/d_steps_hi/mean:2}] steps pooled over tiers (Table~\ref{tab:paired}). The oracle is at most a few steps ahead of the symbolic channel (\rhval{paired/oracle-minus-symbolic/all/d_steps/mean:2} [\rhval{paired/oracle-minus-symbolic/all/d_steps_lo/mean:2}, \rhval{paired/oracle-minus-symbolic/all/d_steps_hi/mean:2}] pooled), so the headroom above both real channels is small in this world.

\subsection{H1: language versus symbolic at equal message budget}
\textbf{Not supported for time-to-find; small gain in success.} Pooled over the four tiers, the NL team takes \rhval{paired/nl-minus-symbolic/all/d_steps/mean:2} [\rhval{paired/nl-minus-symbolic/all/d_steps_lo/mean:2}, \rhval{paired/nl-minus-symbolic/all/d_steps_hi/mean:2}] \emph{more} steps than the symbolic team, an interval that excludes zero on the side of the symbolic channel. Its success rate is higher by \rhval{paired/nl-minus-symbolic/all/d_success/mean:pct1} [\rhval{paired/nl-minus-symbolic/all/d_success_lo/mean:pct1}, \rhval{paired/nl-minus-symbolic/all/d_success_hi/mean:pct1}] percentage points (Table~\ref{tab:pairedsucc}), an interval that barely excludes zero. A seed-level paired $t$-test on the pooled task agrees on the direction for steps (NL \rhval{main/nl-messaging/all/steps/mean:2} against symbolic \rhval{main/symbolic-messaging/all/steps/mean:2} steps, $p=\,$\rhval{cmp/main/symbolic-messaging/all/steps/paired_p:3}, 5 seeds). Both effects are small against the seed-to-seed spread in Table~\ref{tab:main}, and the NL channel uses about twice as many tokens. We therefore do not find that the language channel yields both higher success and lower time-to-find; on time it loses slightly.

\subsection{H2: dependence on the query tier}
\textbf{Supported in direction, with a reversal for closed queries.} For exact class names the NL team is slower by \rhval{paired/nl-minus-symbolic/exact/d_steps/mean:2} [\rhval{paired/nl-minus-symbolic/exact/d_steps_lo/mean:2}, \rhval{paired/nl-minus-symbolic/exact/d_steps_hi/mean:2}] steps and for synonyms by \rhval{paired/nl-minus-symbolic/synonym/d_steps/mean:2} [\rhval{paired/nl-minus-symbolic/synonym/d_steps_lo/mean:2}, \rhval{paired/nl-minus-symbolic/synonym/d_steps_hi/mean:2}] steps; both intervals exclude zero. Two details qualify the hypothesis. First, the synonym tier behaves like the exact tier: a synonym makes the detector less sharp for both channels alike, and the language channel has no extra way to exploit it, because both receivers match the reported class against the query with the same scorer. Second, the language channel never becomes clearly \emph{faster} than the symbolic one; on the open tiers it roughly breaks even on time and wins on success.
