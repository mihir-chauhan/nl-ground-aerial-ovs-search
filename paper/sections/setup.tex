\textbf{Scale.} This is a small CPU study in a toy world with scripted agents. All experiments together took about half an hour on two threads of a shared machine.

\textbf{Systems.} \emph{NL messaging} is the method under test. The baselines, all reimplemented here on the same agents, are: \emph{Symbolic messaging}; \emph{Frontier team, no comm} (the same two frontier robots without a channel, in the tradition of multi-robot frontier exploration~\cite{xu2022explore}); \emph{Single ground frontier}; \emph{Random-walk team} (both robots move randomly, the ground robot still walks to and verifies its own detections); and \emph{Oracle sharing}, in which sighting records are passed losslessly at every step with no entry limit. The oracle bounds the channel, not the task: it still shares only what the aerial robot perceived.

\textbf{Tasks and seeds.} The main comparison runs 40 episodes per query tier and seed, with 5 seeds, for every system. Ablation sweeps use a \emph{mixed} task whose episodes cycle through the four tiers, with 80 episodes per seed. Layout, sensor noise and corruption noise depend only on seed, tier and episode index, so all systems face the same worlds and the same percepts (common random numbers), and episodes can be paired.

\textbf{Metrics.} \emph{Success} within the step cap; \emph{steps} to find, set to the cap when the target is not found; an SPL-style score~\cite{anderson2018evaluation} (success times the shortest ground path from start to target divided by the steps taken); \emph{tokens} per episode (words for NL, fields for symbolic); and \emph{overlap}, the Jaccard index of the cells ever sensed by the two robots.

\textbf{Statistics.} Tables give mean and standard deviation over seeds. Because seeds are few, the decisive comparisons are paired at the episode level: for two systems we take the per-episode difference on identical layouts and report its mean with a 95\% percentile bootstrap interval (10000 resamples of episodes) and a sign-flip permutation test.

\textbf{Development and tuning.} Separate development seeds were used for debugging and to choose the step cap, which was set so that the single ground robot succeeds in roughly half of the episodes; with a cap twice as long every frontier system succeeds almost always and success carries no information. Detector, sensing and channel parameters were fixed on plausibility before the evaluation seeds were run and were not changed afterwards. No parameter is tuned per system.
