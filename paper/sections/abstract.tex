Heterogeneous robot teams are increasingly coordinated through natural language, but it is rarely tested whether the language format itself helps compared with a fixed message schema. We build a small scripted grid-world testbed for open-vocabulary object search by a ground robot and an aerial robot in which only the message channel changes: templated English sentences read by a rule-based parser (object, colour, nearby landmark, room name) versus fixed-schema tuples (class id and coordinates), at the same message budget and on identical layouts. Over four query tiers and 5 seeds, the natural-language team was not faster than the symbolic team: pooled over tiers it needed \rhval{paired/nl-minus-symbolic/all/d_steps/mean:2} [\rhval{paired/nl-minus-symbolic/all/d_steps_lo/mean:2}, \rhval{paired/nl-minus-symbolic/all/d_steps_hi/mean:2}] more steps (paired mean, 95\% bootstrap interval), with a small gain in success of \rhval{paired/nl-minus-symbolic/all/d_success/mean:pct1} [\rhval{paired/nl-minus-symbolic/all/d_success_lo/mean:pct1}, \rhval{paired/nl-minus-symbolic/all/d_success_hi/mean:pct1}] percentage points. The difference depends on the query as hypothesised: language was slower for exact and synonym queries and reached higher success for relational queries. A decomposition explains both effects. Attribute and relation content helps regardless of format, since a schema with two extra fields behaves identically to a sentence with coordinates, and locating objects by room name instead of coordinates costs \rhval{paired/nl-minus-nl+coords/all/d_steps/mean:2} [\rhval{paired/nl-minus-nl+coords/all/d_steps_lo/mean:2}, \rhval{paired/nl-minus-nl+coords/all/d_steps_hi/mean:2}] steps. Under message corruption both channels degrade and the symbolic one does not degrade less. Communication of either kind matters clearly more than the choice of format: the step difference to the same team without a channel is \rhval{paired/symbolic-minus-no-comm/all/d_steps/mean:2} (symbolic) and \rhval{paired/nl-minus-no-comm/all/d_steps/mean:2} (language). The study is a CPU-only toy with a templated language channel; it tests the information-format hypothesis, not the behaviour of language models.
